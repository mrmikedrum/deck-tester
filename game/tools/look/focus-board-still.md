Focus: the board standing still. Look only at the settled stills (`stage-*.jpg` and the per-seat crops), card by card, as a player at arm's length with a designer's eye. The board is everything drawn on the table: cards, standees, piles and their counts, pages and their borders, plaques, labels and signs, glows and marks.
- cards overlapping or clipping into each other, into labels, plaques or page borders; anything cut off by the screen edge or a panel;
- names, numbers, counts and labels that are unreadable, truncated, colliding, or set in mismatched sizes;
- alignment and spacing: ragged rows, uneven gaps, things almost but not quite lined up, crowding in one place and emptiness in another;
- consistency: the same kind of thing drawn two ways; colours, borders, shadows or lighting that disagree; anything that looks like a bug or placeholder;
- what a player can tell at a glance: each opponent's creatures, then their other permanents; what is tapped; what a pile holds.
The layout audit measures some of this; a breach is a finding, and so is anything it can't measure. Ignore motion and the HTML around the board.
