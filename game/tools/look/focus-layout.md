Focus: the board as a game fills it. The `game` clip steps one game from turn 2 to turn 15, then leans in on one seat and opens a pile. Judge each stage's still and its per-seat crops as a player holding an iPad at arm's length:
- Can I tell what each opponent has that matters (creatures first, then other permanents), and what I have, at a glance?
- Can I read each card's name, and does every card and pile look like something I can tap? Is it clear which things are piles and how many they hold?
- Do cards cover each other, labels collide, or things spill off their page? Is space used well, or crammed in one place and empty in another?
- Does it stay calm and recognisable from stage to stage: does a card I saw last turn stay where it was?
- When I lean in and open a pile, can I see and read everything in it?
The layout audit's hard limits are not negotiable; say where each breach shows on screen and what it costs a player.
