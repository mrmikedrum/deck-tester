Focus: everything a player would call unpolished, standing still and in motion. Spend about half your attention on each.

**Still.** Every settled moment has a full-resolution still (`stage-*.png`) and a crop per seat (`stage-*-<seat>.jpg`). Look at each closely, card by card, as a player at arm's length with a designer's eye:
- cards overlapping or clipping into each other, into labels, plaques or page borders; anything cut off by the screen edge or a panel;
- text: names, numbers and labels that are unreadable, truncated, colliding, or set in mismatched sizes;
- alignment and spacing: ragged rows, uneven gaps, things almost but not quite lined up, crowding in one place and emptiness in another;
- consistency: the same kind of thing drawn two ways; colours, borders, shadows or lighting that disagree; anything that looks like a bug, a placeholder or debug output;
- hierarchy: what draws the eye first, and whether that is what matters.
The layout audit measures some of this (sizes, taps, what covers what); a breach is a finding, and so is anything it can't measure.

**In motion.** Follow every card and page that moves, from hand to stack, stack to battlefield or graveyard, tokens arriving, attackers and blockers, things leaving. Narrate first, citing frames; compare adjacent frames part by part. Then judge against real cards on a real table and a player trying to follow the game: pops, snaps, teleports, paths nothing real would take, passing through other things, arriving before or after the prompt says so, moving without reason, or leaving the player unsure what happened, whose it was, or what it targeted.
