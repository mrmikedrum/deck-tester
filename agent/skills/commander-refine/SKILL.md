# Refine

Read the relevant projects' visions and state, [principles](../../docs/partner-principles.md) and relevant preferences (decks repository: `docs/mikes-deckbuilding-preferences.md`). Consult consolidated notes for relevant decisions, exclusions and open ideas. Follow any local persistence/sync workflow named by the decks repository for pulling edits, draft scope, major-pivot backups and verified publication. Match depth to the question; iteration ends with a decision brief, not a mandatory full-deck report. This workflow supports any number of decks, not a particular project or paired-deck structure.

Refinement is an open loop: combine [exploration](../commander-explore/SKILL.md), [search](../commander-search/SKILL.md), draft edits/copies and [goldfishing](../commander-goldfish/SKILL.md) in whatever order helps. Test staged lists before publishing. Use goldfishing when actual sequencing can answer a consequential question about mana, curve or engine assembly, then revise as needed. It is available during the pass, not a mandatory gate.

Resolve missing or disputed card facts with [search](../commander-search/SKILL.md). Distinguish playing list from candidates, including real/proxy quantities and commanders. Bound incomplete analysis explicitly.

Find the bottleneck: development, color access, engine availability, card flow, interaction, recovery, setup or finish. Evaluate role timing/dependencies and unique slots separately; conditional draw, selection, rituals and late engines differ from reliable infrastructure. Review early colors/tapped-land costs alongside spells; pip ratios alone are insufficient. Role/land ranges are starting hypotheses, adjustable for commander, curve, strategy and pod.

For substantive passes, use [search](../commander-search/SKILL.md) to challenge the existing pool before settling on swaps. Search the desired outcome and missing role, not just the current engine's keyword: complementary Oracle wording, confirmed functional/art tags and adjacent resource or trigger interactions can reveal different solutions. Recently researched candidates remain useful, but repeated recycling or a persistent bottleneck calls for a new angle. Exact-name verification alone is not discovery. Respect explicit pool restrictions and keep prescribed edits narrow.

Compare promising outsiders against incumbents on fit, usable floor, dependencies and table experience. Apply Mike's thematic resonance standard (decks repository: `docs/mikes-deckbuilding-preferences.md`) to names, intended art and identity as well as mechanics, for both additions and existing cards in each branch; structural improvements must still belong in the deck. Keeping the old card is valid when it wins that comparison; overlap alone proves neither stagnation nor quality. Briefly surface a consequential discovery or the reason searched alternatives lost, rather than delivering a search report. Stop when the question is adequately tested or new routes stop adding useful options; disclose unavailable search instead of implying it happened.

Record each meaningful deckbuilding decision as it happens (using the active persistence plugin's refinement trail when configured): finding/evidence, decision, reason, credible alternatives, tradeoffs and checks; distinguish predictions from measured results. Explain counts through usable timing, conditions and deck needs rather than nominal totals. A generic staple should face a thematic alternative. Preserve protected choices and explain conflicts.

Before finalizing, consider the sum of the decisions against each deck's vision, fun and preferences: does the combination still make sense, or has it drifted? Revise when needed. Structural checks do not validate colored mana: check actual sources, fetch targets, timing and competing costs against spell requirements, or explicitly mark that review incomplete.

Review the combined play pattern: visible/disruptable finish, agency, turn length, repetitive tutors/loops, recovery and political pile-ons. Basic structural checks are not a bracket/social certificate. [Failure checks](../../docs/guide-failure-modes.md) provide a quick diagnostic.

Use real-game feedback and simulator observations to test explanations against the project's intended experience. Identify the list revision, mulligans and relevant context; distinguish deck problems from piloting/tool errors and unusual draws. Seek repeated evidence and consider competing explanations: one missed land drop does not establish too few lands. Goldfishing tests development and sequencing; actual games and opponents' own feedback inform interaction, recovery and social experience. Record consequential findings and the next useful experiment in refinement decisions or consolidated notes.

Deliver the decisions directly as the primary chat response: decision, why, important alternatives/tradeoffs, and a short whole-pass assessment. Lightly consolidate superseded choices while keeping consequential reconsiderations visible; reasoning belongs in chat, not behind an attachment or rewritten into a separate essay. Distill future-relevant conclusions into project notes. Use the [original guide](../../docs/refinement-guide.md) only for deeper structural reference.
